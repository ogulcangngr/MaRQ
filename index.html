    Surface analysis
    Feature
    Automatic finds whether the scan holds a channel or a ridge, as on a mould master, and turns a ridge over into the channel it moulds. Choose Channel or Ridge when it decides wrongly, as for a channel whose rims rise higher than it is deep. The choice goes back to Automatic when another file is opened. How it decides
    Voxel size (0 = automatic)
    Grid cell size for downsampling; 0 uses the larger side of the scan divided by 1000.
    Field band (× median absolute deviation)
    How tightly the flat field plane is fitted. Lower is a stricter field selection. Levelling and orientation are referenced to this field.
    Crop margin along the channel
    Extra distance beyond the detected channel length at each end of the crop box, in micrometres.
    Crop margin across the channel
    Extra distance beyond the detected channel width on each side of the crop box, in micrometres.
    Opening level (fraction of depth) 0.10
    Depth fraction where the opening width and length are read. 0.10 is the top 10 % of the depth, close to where the channel meets the flat surface.
    Floor level (fraction of depth) 0.90
    Depth fraction for the floor width, the channel bottom where the nominal width is defined. 0.90 is the bottom 10 % of the depth.
    Floor roughness strip width (fraction) 0.50
    Width of the floor roughness strip as a fraction of the channel width (its central part).
    Floor roughness strip length
    Length of the floor roughness strip along the channel, centred on the channel.
    Field roughness strip width
    Fixed width of the left and right field strips. Their position is found automatically, just past the raised channel lip, on clean flat field.
    Field roughness strip length
    Fixed length of the field strips along the channel, centred on the channel.
    Advanced
    Edge margin
    Distance from each edge of the scan discarded before the analysis, removing boundary artefacts.
    Depth profile slices
    Number of slices along the channel for the local depth and its spread (the widths and the length are read on grid lines).
    Comparison
    Grid spacing (µm)
    Cell size of the common grid. RQd is defined at a stated cell size; keep the same value for every comparison so the correlations stay comparable.
    Smoothing before the slopes (µm)
    Gaussian blur before the slopes are computed. At least one grid cell is always applied, for stable slopes.
    Display of the surface analysis
    Display cell size (0 = automatic)
    Side of the square cells of the height maps and the 3D view, in micrometres. Display only: the geometry and the roughness are computed from the points. 0 chooses the finest cell (never below the voxel size) that keeps each map within 160 000 cells.
    Display smoothing (cells)
    Gaussian blur radius on the display grids, in cells; 0 switches smoothing off.
    Reading the files
    Header rows to skip
    Lines at the top of each point cloud file to leave out, in the analysis and in the comparison. With 0, lines of text at the top (column names, instrument data) are left out by themselves.
    Changed values are marked and remembered on this computer. The paper defaults are the values used in the paper.
    Channel or ridge

    MaRQ measures a channel, a depression in the flat surface around it. A ridge, as on a mould master, is first turned over into the channel it moulds. Automatic decides which of the two a scan holds by how far the surface reaches below and above the flat surface, and the larger reach wins.

    The heights alone cannot tell the third surface from a ridge, because turned over it is an ordinary surface too, two grooves beside a raised strip. In the same way, grooves beside a ridge that are deeper than the ridge is high make it look like a channel, so choose Ridge for such a scan.

    The choice holds for the file open now and goes back to Automatic when another file is opened. If nothing lies on the chosen side, MaRQ says so after the analysis.

    Interpret the comparison

    Choose what the two surfaces are. MaRQ then shows the values to read for that pair, as the paper does, and how each compares with a reference.

    Which values to read

    Which surfaces are compared, and which values are read, depends on the question, as in the paper's figure of the assessment tasks. The tool is the mould, master or stamp.

    MaRQ sets no acceptance thresholds: tolerances depend on the device and its function. After a comparison, Interpret shows the values of a pair.

    Export a figure
    At a journal size the figure is drawn at its printed size, with its text near 7 to 9 points and without the title of the view; a PNG is made at the chosen resolution, an SVG as vectors.
    The figure is rebuilt from the result in the chosen theme and size, with axes and a colour bar, so labels and line widths stay legible in a document. For a picture of the 3D viewport as it is on screen, use the camera button below the view.

    About MaRQ
    MaRQ
    Microchannel and Replication Quality Analyzer, version 1.0

    MaRQ measures microchannels in optical-profilometry point clouds and compares two surfaces of a channel, A and B, with the height correlation RQ, the directional slope correlations RQdx and RQdy, and RQd, the slope-magnitude correlation over the channel interior.

    Please cite

    The metrics
    RQ: Pearson correlation of the two height maps over the channel-interior mask. RQdx, RQdy: correlations of the signed slopes along and across the channel. RQd: correlation of the slope-magnitude maps at the stated grid cell size (5 µm in the paper); it is the grading metric because it responds to sidewall shape where height correlation saturates.
